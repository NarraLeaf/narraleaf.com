---
title: "JumpConfig"
---
```ts
type JumpConfig = {
    transition: Transition;
    returnable: boolean;
};
```

## transition

The transition played while [`Scene.jumpTo`](/docs/narraleaf-react/core/elements/scene#jumpto) swaps the scenes.

Since `0.27.0` it is played across the whole stage rather than across the background alone. Both scenes are on screen for the length of the jump: the outgoing scene drives the transition's outgoing half and the incoming scene its incoming half, so sprites, text and every other layer take part.

```typescript
scene.jumpTo(nextScene, new Reveal({duration: 800, pattern: Mask.iris()}));
```

For the list of transitions, see [Transition](/docs/narraleaf-react/core/animation/transition).

Two things follow from a transition covering the stage:

- Its geometry is the stage, not the background image. A `Push` travels the width of the stage, and a `Mask` is laid over the stage rectangle. A background deliberately smaller than the stage is swept along with everything else rather than being the thing swept.
- The dialog box takes no part in it. It is rendered outside the stage and is gone once the scene ends.

The field is typed `Transition` rather than `ImageTransition`. Every built-in transition satisfies both, so calls written before `0.27.0` are unaffected.

Whether the skip key skips this transition is governed by [`allowSkipSceneTransition`](/docs/narraleaf-react/core/types/GameConfig).

## returnable

Come back here when the target scene runs out of actions, instead of leaving for good. Added in `0.39.0`; defaults to `false`, which is the plain jump `jumpTo` has always performed.

```typescript
scene.jumpTo(storeroom, {returnable: true});
```

With `true` the calling scene is **suspended** rather than unloaded. It keeps its stage, its sprites, its layers and its scene-local variables, its background music is paused, and it stops painting while the target scene is on screen. When the target scene runs out of actions the call returns: the target is unloaded, the calling scene paints again, its music resumes from where it was paused, and the action after the jump runs.

Only the scene's own background music is suspended. Everything else a sound plays through belongs to the story rather than to one scene, and plays on across a call exactly as it plays on across a plain jump.

A scene cannot be called while it is already on the call stack: one `Scene` owns one place on the stage and one set of scene-local variables, so it cannot be in two of them at once. Calling one that is already there throws. How many scenes a chain of calls may keep suspended at once is [`maxSceneCallDepth`](/docs/narraleaf-react/core/types/GameConfig), which defaults to `8`.
